## Claim

*C. elegans* magnetic orientation, attributed to the AFD neuron pair, is gated by radical load in the tissue. Part of that load comes from sub-keV nuclear recoils caused by a strongly interacting dark-matter subcomponent, so orientation strength should follow sidereal time.

## Mechanism

1. **Dark particle:** a ~1 GeV particle with nucleon cross-section σ ≈ 10⁻²⁸ cm², making up a fraction f of the halo, arrives from the direction of Cygnus (conjecture).
2. **Recoil:** it scatters off hydrogen in the worm, giving proton recoils of ≤0.5 keV, each leaving roughly 10 ionisations and radical pairs along a ~10 nm track.
3. **Chemistry:** the radicals raise H₂O₂ and feed spin-correlated radical pairs, whose recombination yield depends on the geomagnetic field (plausible, but unproven in worms).
4. **Nerve signal:** AFD's cGMP cascade (guanylyl cyclases, TAX-4 channel) is redox-modulated, so radical load sets the gain of its magnetic response (conjecture).
5. **Behaviour:** the worm's burrowing direction relative to the field sharpens or blurs.

The AFD magnetic sense itself (Vidal-Gadea 2015) is contested, since Landler 2018 failed to replicate it.

## Size of the effect

- **Dark rate:** about 7·f recoils per worm-hour (my estimate, assuming a flux of 7×10⁶ cm⁻² s⁻¹ and a 4 µg worm), or ≤3·f keV per hour.
- **Ordinary background:** 1 mGy/yr deposits about 3 keV per worm-hour, so the dark dose only matches it at f ≈ 1.
- **Constraint:** f ≈ 1 is excluded by surface and rocket detectors, and at the allowed f ≲ 10⁻³ the dark dose is ≤0.1% of background.
- **Noise:** metabolic ROS exceeds either dose by many orders of magnitude, so the hypothesis needs track-clustered radicals to matter disproportionately. This is its weakest link.

## What we would observe

- **Sidereal rhythm:** the Earth is opaque at this σ, so flux collapses when Cygnus sets. The orientation index should cycle with a 23.93 h period, not 24.00 h.
- **Depth:** the effect should vanish under about 1 km of rock (optical depth ~20), but not under 10 cm of lead.

## How to test it

- **Surface assay:** run burrowing assays in agar-filled pipettes inside a Helmholtz cage, with 50 worms per run every 2 hours for at least 6 months, to separate sidereal from solar periods.
- **Blinding and controls:** score blind, and include *tax-4* and AFD-ablated strains as controls.
- **Underground replicate:** repeat at Gran Sasso or SNOLAB, with a ¹³⁷Cs source and radon restoring the surface gamma dose.
- **Existing data:** re-analyse the timestamps of the Vidal-Gadea and Landler assays against local sidereal time.

## What would count against it

- No sidereal component above about 5% of the orientation index.
- A rhythm that locks to solar time.
- Underground orientation fully restored by adding gamma dose back.
- Orientation unchanged by tenfold changes in radiation dose.

## Claim

Sub-keV nuclear recoils from a strongly interacting dark-matter subcomponent add to the whole-body oxidative state of *C. elegans*, and magnetic orientation is at most a downstream readout of that state. The earlier claim, that the dark dose gates AFD directly, is dropped: AFD takes under 10⁻⁶ hits per assay-hour.

## Mechanism

1. **Particle (conjecture):** about 1 GeV, σ ≈ 10⁻²⁸ cm² per nucleon, halo fraction f ≲ 10⁻³.
2. **Recoil:** the main channel is coherent scattering on oxygen at ≤70 eV, which breaks bonds without ionising. Proton recoils of ≤0.5 keV are rarer.
3. **Chemistry (conjecture):** clustered bond breaks trigger oxidative-stress signalling more strongly than their share of total radicals would suggest.
4. **Behaviour (conjecture):** redox state alters the gain of AFD's cGMP cascade. The AFD magnetic sense itself remains contested (Vidal-Gadea 2015; Landler 2018).

## Size of the effect

- **Rate:** about 0.7·f proton recoils and about 300·f oxygen recoils per worm-hour. At f = 10⁻³ that is roughly one oxygen recoil per worm every three hours.
- **Noise:** metabolic ROS is about 10¹¹ molecules per worm-hour. The gap is about 10¹³, and track clustering cannot plausibly bridge it.
- **Consequence:** the hypothesis survives only if worms respond measurably to about one recoil per hour. That is a measurable number, so it is measured first.

## What we would observe

- **Lead:** 10 cm of lead (optical depth about 5) removes most of the dark flux, so oxidative reporters should shift inside lead even with the gamma dose restored.
- **Sidereal rhythm:** this is weak at northern sites. The atmosphere (optical depth about 3) isotropises the flux, and the apex at declination +48° barely sets. It is only worth pursuing at a low-latitude site.
- **Neutrons:** reporter and orientation responses should scale with keV recoil rate, and not with matched gamma dose.

## How to test it

1. **Lead on/off:** described below.
2. **Neutron calibration:** use an Sb-Be source (about 24 keV) to give 10 to 10⁵ recoils per worm-hour, with a dose-matched gamma arm. The slope gives the reachable f.
3. **Sidereal series:** run this only if steps 1 and 2 are positive.
   - **Site and duration:** at least 12 months at a low-latitude site.
   - **Artefact check:** require a null at the anti-sidereal period (24.07 h).
   - **Logging:** record temperature to millikelvin, plus magnetometer and RF.
   - **Worm state:** fix feeding state and age.
   - **Controls:** score blind, with *tax-4* and AFD-ablated strains.

## What would count against it

- No reporter difference between lead and sham at the few-percent level.
- No response at 10⁵ neutron recoils per worm-hour, which makes any sidereal search moot.
- A response that follows gamma dose as closely as recoil rate.
- A rhythm at the solar period, or equal power at the sidereal and anti-sidereal periods.

## Cheapest first experiment

Alternate age-synchronised *gst-4::GFP* worms, blind, between two enclosures for 24 h exposures over a few weeks.

- **Lead arm:** a 10 cm lead enclosure, with KCl inside to restore the gamma dose.
- **Sham arm:** a thermally matched plastic enclosure, which is transparent to the particle (optical depth about 0.02, my estimate).
- **Logging:** temperature to millikelvin in both.
- **Readout:** whole-worm fluorescence, with burrowing orientation as a secondary measure.
- **Known confound:** lead also changes the cosmic-ray secondary field, since muons produce neutrons in it. A positive result therefore needs the neutron arm to interpret it.
- **Decision:** a null result kills the chain at any allowed f, with no accelerator, mine or year-long series needed.
