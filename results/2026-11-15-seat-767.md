## Claim
Conjecture: a dark photon that propagates in an extra dimension of size R ≈ 0.1–0.3 µm has a Kaluza-Klein tower of masses m_n ≈ n·ħc/R, which puts several modes at 1.8–4 eV. If these modes are dark matter, rhodopsin absorbs them like ordinary photons. The 12 narrow-band channels of the mantis shrimp would sample that comb of lines, and its polarisation receptors would read the field's fixed direction in space.

## Mechanism
1. Established physics, given the particle: dark-photon dark matter with kinetic mixing ε is absorbed at ε² times the rate of a photon of energy m_n c².
2. Absorption isomerises retinal and produces a quantum bump identical to a real photon.
3. Each mode loads only the channel whose band contains m_n.
4. The field's polarisation vector is fixed in the galactic frame (conjecture). Paired receptors with orthogonal microvilli, including the cells under the quarter-wave R8 retarder in rows 5–6, are then modulated in antiphase once per sidereal day.

## Size of the effect
- **Signal:** the equivalent flux is n·c·ε² ≈ 5×10¹⁸ ε² per cm² per second. At the allowed ε ≈ 10⁻¹², a 10⁻⁷ cm² rhabdom gets about 10⁻¹² events per second.
- **Noise:** thermal isomerisations run at roughly 10⁻² per second per cell (the toad-rod value; unmeasured in stomatopods).
- **Verdict:** the signal sits about 10 orders below the noise, so the animal cannot perceive it. The retina is useful only as a detector design.

## What we would observe
- A dark-bump excess in some colour channels and not others, with channel energies in integer ratios.
- A difference in rate between orthogonal receptor pairs that oscillates at 1.0027 cycles per day.

## How to test it
- **Data:** dark-adapted intracellular or electroretinogram recordings from midband rows 1–6, in shielded darkness at constant temperature, for at least 12 months. That length is needed to separate the sidereal from the solar frequency.
- **Null hypothesis:** the pair-difference rate has zero amplitude at the sidereal frequency.
- **Statistic:** the harmonic amplitude of that difference, which cancels common-mode thermal drift.
- **Controls:**
  - the anti-sidereal frequency (0.9973 cycles per day), where no physical signal can exist;
  - the solar frequency, to catch circadian leakage;
  - fixed retina or an opsin-free preparation;
  - a rotated mount, since the phase must follow the microvillar axis;
  - analysis blinded by a hidden time offset.
- **Sample size:** 10³ cells for one year gives 3×10¹⁰ cell-seconds and about 3×10⁸ background events. A 5σ detection then needs about 10⁵ modulated events, which corresponds to ε ≈ 2×10⁻⁹.
- **Correction:** use Bonferroni across the 12 channels and 3 test frequencies.
- **Buildable sensor:** a stacked, polarisation-resolved skipper CCD copying the midband layout has 10⁶ times lower dark rate and could reach untested ε.

## What would count against it
- Existing limits already do: direct-detection and solar bounds (ε ≲ 10⁻¹² at a few eV) rule out the biological retina's reach by about three orders.
- Any amplitude at the anti-sidereal frequency equal to the sidereal one.
- A phase that does not follow mount rotation.
- An excess with no integer comb across channels.
- A null from the CCD analogue down to ε ≈ 10⁻¹³.

--- critique:experimentalist · claude (claude CLI) ---
**1. Weakest link: the recording, then the comb.**
- **Recording (established limit):** intracellular impalements last minutes to hours, and an electroretinogram cannot resolve single bumps. So 10³ cells for a year really means about 10⁵–10⁶ separate short impalements, each with its own gain, dark rate and microvillar angle. Variance between cells, not Poisson noise, will set the floor, so ε ≈ 2×10⁻⁹ is optimistic.
- **Comb:** the narrow channels come from filter pigments and tiering, which attenuate real photons. A dark photon passes through them unattenuated, so each cell responds with its bare opsin band, about 0.5 eV wide. For R = 0.3 µm the mode spacing is ħc/R ≈ 0.66 eV, so the retina barely resolves adjacent modes and the integer-ratio test is weak.

**2. Ordinary explanations to exclude.**
- **Temperature:** thermal isomerisation changes by roughly 10–15% per kelvin (vertebrate-rod value). The 5σ threshold is a fractional modulation of 3×10⁻⁴, so the pair members must be matched to a few millikelvin.
- **Circadian control of retinal noise:** established in Limulus, and an annually modulated solar-day signal leaks into both sidebands.
- **Stray light from the preparation:** Cherenkov flashes from ⁴⁰K decays in saline and tissue, and ultraweak chemiluminescence from oxidising lipids.
- **Cosmic muons:** their rate has small solar and sidereal harmonics of its own.
- **Preparation rundown:** if it is correlated with time of day through the lab schedule.

**3. One change: rotate the detector.**
Mount the polarisation-resolved skipper CCD on a turntable with a period of about 10 minutes. Absorption goes as cos²θ, so the signal appears at exactly twice the rotation frequency, far from daily drifts. Its amplitude and phase must then trace a sidereal pattern over days rather than 12 months. A counter-rotating or stationary twin serves as the null.

**1. Weakest link: the sidereal modulation, and the reach.**
- **Polarisation coherence:** virialised dark-photon dark matter at 2.5 eV has a coherence time of h/(mv²) ≈ 2 ns and a coherence length of about 0.5 mm. Unless the polarisation is fixed cosmologically (a model-dependent assumption), it randomises every few nanoseconds and the 1.0027 cycles/day signal averages to zero.
- **Reach:** the 5σ arithmetic is right (ε ≈ 2×10⁻⁹), but that is about 10⁶ in rate above existing bounds. It also assumes Poisson counts, whereas bump rates drift and are overdispersed.
- **Recording:** intracellular recordings last hours, not a year, and an electroretinogram cannot resolve single bumps.
- **Tower bound:** the Sun emits every Kaluza-Klein mode up to about 1 keV, roughly 10³ modes, so the solar bound on ε tightens by about √N ≈ 30. The local density is also shared among modes.
- **CCD analogue:** silicon absorbs isotropically, so it has no polarisation handle. I also believe skipper CCDs (SENSEI, DAMIC-M) and FUNK already cover ε ≲ 10⁻¹² above 1.2 eV, so "untested" needs checking.

**2. Ordinary explanations to exclude.**
- Efferent circadian control of dark noise, which is large in Limulus. Its annual amplitude modulation puts sidebands at both the sidereal and anti-sidereal frequencies.
- Temperature cycles.
- Cherenkov light from muons and ⁴⁰K.
- Tissue chemiluminescence from lipid peroxidation.
- Electrode drift.

**3. One change: test the comb, not the animal.**
The model has one free parameter, R, which fixes every line: m_n = n × 0.197 eV·µm / R. Dark-matter lines are narrow (ΔE/E ≈ 10⁻⁶), so fit a fixed-spacing template to existing spectrally resolved data (FUNK, LAMPOST-type dielectric stacks, skipper CCD spectra), scanning R over 0.1–0.3 µm.
- **Null:** no excess at the comb positions.
- **Control:** scrambled, non-integer spacings.
- **Gain:** stacking N modes improves sensitivity by about √N and reduces the look-elsewhere penalty to a single scanned parameter.
- **Polarisation:** layered stacks respond only to the field component in the layer plane, so two orthogonal stacks reproduce the antiphase pair test.

## Claim
Conjecture: a dark photon in a flat extra dimension of size R ≈ 0.1–0.3 µm has Kaluza-Klein masses m_n = n × 0.197 eV·µm / R, which puts 2–6 narrow lines (ΔE/E ≈ 10⁻⁶) at 1.8–4 eV. The mantis shrimp cannot perceive them, but its midband layout is the right detector design.

## Mechanism
1. Given the particle, absorption at ε² times the photon rate is established physics.
2. The 12-channel comb reading is dropped: filter pigments do not attenuate dark photons, so each cell responds with its bare opsin band (about 0.5 eV).
3. A mirror restores it by converting dark photons into real photons of energy m_n, which narrow-band filters and polarisers then sort.
4. Sidereal antiphase is demoted: the polarisation randomises every 2 ns unless it is fixed cosmologically.

## Size of the effect
- **Animal:** at most 10⁻¹² events per second per rhabdom, against about 10⁻² thermal. The solar bound over about 10³ modes tightens ε by about 30, so the gap is about 13 orders.
- **Mirror:** the photon rate is about 5×10¹⁸ ε² per cm² per second. A 1 m² mirror at ε = 10⁻¹³ gives about 5×10⁻⁴ per second, comparable to superconducting-sensor dark rates (10⁻⁵–10⁻⁴ per second, to be confirmed).

## What we would observe
- Excess counts at integer-spaced energies fixed by the single parameter R.
- Only if the polarisation is fixed: a signal at twice the rotation frequency behind a rotating polariser, with sidereal phase drift.

## How to test it
- **Data:** energy-resolved single-photon spectra with resolution of 0.2 eV or better. Skipper CCDs and FUNK do not resolve 1.8–4 eV, so the theorist's reanalysis targets fail there.
- **Null hypothesis:** zero amplitude for the comb template at every R.
- **Statistic:** Poisson profile-likelihood ratio, maximised over R, with a smooth fitted background.
- **Look-elsewhere:** calibrate the global p-value with 10⁴ pseudo-experiments.
- **Controls:**
  - scrambled non-integer spacings with the same line count;
  - mirror blocked or defocused;
  - blinded energy offset;
  - a stationary twin for the rotation test.
- **Stacking gain:** at most √6 ≈ 2.4 in this band, and it is offset because the modes share the local density.
- **Sample size:** with B background counts in the comb windows, 5σ global needs about 6√B signal counts. For B = 100 that is about 60 events.
- **Overdispersion:** estimate it from off-comb sidebands and inflate the variance.

## What would count against it
- The scrambled combs fit as well as the integer comb.
- The excess survives with the mirror blocked.
- The best-fit R differs between detectors.
- The 2f signal appears in the stationary twin.
- A mirror null down to ε ≈ 10⁻¹³ across R = 0.1–0.3 µm.

## Cheapest first experiment
Fit the comb template to an existing dark-count energy spectrum from an optical transition-edge sensor (about 10⁶ s). It needs no hardware; I have not checked which datasets are public.

For a sensor about 25 µm across, I estimate a reach of only ε ≈ 10⁻⁹, which is already excluded. The run therefore validates the statistic, the trials factor and the scrambled-comb control. New ground needs the mirror.
