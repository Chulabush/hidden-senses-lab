## Claim
In polar twilight, UV/blue light drives cryptochrome radical pairs in the reindeer's winter retina. An axion-like particle (ALP) with mass near 6×10⁻⁹ eV would act on those electron spins as a radio-frequency pseudo-magnetic field. That would modulate UV contrast along the "axion wind" direction. This is conjecture, and my estimate says it fails by about 12 orders of magnitude.

## Mechanism
1. **Coupling:** L = (g_aee/2mₑ) ∂_μa ψ̄γ^μγ⁵ψ gives H = (g_aee/2mₑ) σ·∇a, which is equivalent to B_eff = g_aee∇a/e.
2. **Field size:** for halo dark matter, |∇a| = v√(2ρ), with ρ = 0.4 GeV/cm³ and v ≈ 10⁻³c. So B_eff ≈ g_aee × 4×10⁻⁸ T, oscillating at f = m_a/2π.
3. **Resonance:** if f matches the electron Larmor frequency in the Arctic geomagnetic field (about 1.5 MHz at 55 µT), the field mixes singlet and triplet states of the FAD radical pair.
4. **Optics (established):** the winter eye passes UV down to about 320 nm, and the tapetum shifts from gold to blue.
5. **Signal (conjecture):** the changed singlet yield alters cryptochrome signalling and so photoreceptor gain. Photoactive, magnetosensitive cryptochrome in mammalian retina is unproven.

## Size of the effect
- **Field:** stellar-cooling and XENONnT limits give g_aee ≲ 10⁻¹³, so B_eff ≲ 4×10⁻²¹ T.
- **Yield change:** about γB_effτ ≈ (1.8×10¹¹ rad/s/T)(4×10⁻²¹ T)(10⁻⁶ s) ≈ 10⁻¹⁵ per radical pair.
- **Noise to beat:** photon shot noise would need about 10³⁰ absorbed photons, while a twilight retina catches about 10⁹ per second. Thermal spin relaxation and anthropogenic RF (pT to nT) are also far larger.
- **Biological benchmark:** the lowest RF fields reported to disrupt bird compasses are about 1 nT, and that figure is contested.
- **Rescue:** even a local dark-matter clump 10⁶ times denser gains only 10³ in field.

## What we would observe
- UV-contrast sensitivity or heading preference modulated on the sidereal day (23 h 56 min), not the solar day, because the wind points towards Cygnus in the galactic frame.
- An annual modulation of about 7% peaking in June.
- Reindeer are a clean test animal because their circadian rhythm weakens in polar night, so solar-day confounds are smaller.

## How to test it
- **Existing data:** run a Lomb–Scargle analysis on winter GPS-collar and accelerometer tracks (Movebank, Svalbard and Fennoscandian herds). Look for power at the sidereal frequency against the solar frequency, which needs more than 60 days to separate the two.
- **Buildable sensor:** record UV electroretinograms from reindeer under an applied 1.5 MHz field, stepped from 1 pT to 100 nT, to measure the biological threshold B_min. This converts the animal into a limit of g_aee < B_min / 4×10⁻⁸ T.
- **Physics cross-check:** compare with electron-spin comagnetometer and torsion-pendulum limits in the same mass band.

## What would count against it
- No sidereal line above the solar sidebands in the collar data.
- An electroretinogram that is insensitive to RF below about 1 nT. That implies g_aee ≳ 10⁻², which is excluded by 11 orders of magnitude.
- No light-induced radical-pair signal in reindeer cryptochrome in vitro.

Any one of these kills the hypothesis. A positive sidereal signal would more plausibly point to starlight or to geomagnetic and ionospheric tides, which would need excluding first.

**1. Weakest link: the yield change is second order, not first.**
The ALP phase is random relative to each radical pair's birth, and its coherence time is about 0.6 s (Q ≈ 10⁶) against a 1 µs pair lifetime. The linear term therefore averages to zero across the ensemble. What survives is (γB_effτ)² ≈ 10⁻³⁰, which needs about 10⁶⁰ photons, not 10³⁰.

The resonance also buys nothing. The pair's linewidth is 1/τ ≈ 1 MHz, comparable to the Larmor frequency itself, so the effective Q is about 1.

On the bench, an electroretinogram resolves fractional changes of about 10⁻³ to 10⁻⁴ after averaging. That is the real floor.

**2. Ordinary explanations to exclude.**
- **GPS artefact:** the constellation geometry repeats every sidereal day (23 h 56 min), so multipath position errors create a sidereal line in collar data by themselves. Use accelerometer channels only.
- **Sky light:** Milky Way and starlight are sidereal. Aurora emits in the UV (337 and 391 nm) and tracks geomagnetic activity.
- **Tides and schedules:** lunar-day aliasing (24 h 50 min), geomagnetic daily variation, and herding or feeding routines.
- **Electroretinogram artefacts:** RF rectification at the electrodes, tissue heating, and ambient medium-wave broadcast near 1.5 MHz.

**3. One change: heterodyne in vitro before using the animal.**
Put purified cryptochrome (or a flavin–tryptophan model, about 10¹⁷ molecules) in a shielded cell. Apply a strong phase-stable bias field B_bias at 1.5 MHz. The cross term 2B_bias·B_a restores a linear response to the weak field. Read out fluorescence by I/Q lock-in.

Calibrate B_min with injected pT test tones and a blinded dummy-load sham, then repeat on retinal explants. This gives an honest, quotable limit on g_aee, though I expect it to stay many orders above the stellar bounds.

- **Sham failure:** the heterodyne signal persists with the dummy load.
- **Already dead:** any claim of behavioural ALP detection.

Run the heterodyne bench test on a commercial flavin–tryptophan model compound, with no animal. Use a mu-metal box, a Helmholtz pair for the static field, an RF coil, a 450 nm LED, a photodiode and a lock-in. Sweep the Larmor resonance and inject pT tones to measure B_min.
