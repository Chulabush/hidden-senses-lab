## Claim
Dark-photon dark matter with mass m ≈ 3.5–3.9 eV (equivalent to 320–350 nm) and kinetic mixing ε is absorbed by reindeer rod rhodopsin as if it were a UV photon. This would add a temperature-independent floor to the rod "dark light" during polar night. The claim is conjecture; my own numbers below say it fails.

## Mechanism
1. **Field:** the local dark matter density of 0.4 GeV/cm³ gives about 10⁸ dark photons per cm³, a flux of about 3×10¹⁸ per cm² per second.
2. **Coupling:** mixing induces an ordinary electric field oscillating at ω = m, scaled by ε.
3. **Absorption:** rhodopsin absorbs it through its UV band, with cross-section σ ≈ 10⁻¹⁶ cm².
4. **Nerve signal:** the resulting isomerisation is indistinguishable from a photon hit, triggering the usual transducin cascade and a single-photon response of about 1 pA.
5. **No optical path:** the field penetrates skull and lens, so the reindeer's UV-transparent optics (established) play no role.

## Size of the effect
- **Rate per molecule:** about 300·ε² per second.
- **At the allowed mixing:** direct-detection and stellar bounds near this mass give ε ≲ 10⁻¹⁵ (recalled, not checked), so the rate is about 3×10⁻²⁸ per second per molecule.
- **Per eye:** with about 10¹⁷ rhodopsin molecules (my rough estimate), that is about 10⁻¹¹ events per second.
- **Noise:** thermal isomerisation runs at about 10⁻² per second per rod, or about 10⁶ per second per eye.
- **Shortfall:** the signal is about 17 orders of magnitude below the noise.

## What we would observe
The rod dark-event rate would follow Arrhenius temperature scaling plus a constant floor. The floor would be identical under lead or mu-metal shielding and proportional to pigment cross-section at energy m, not to species.

## How to test it
- **Recordings:** suction-electrode recordings from isolated rods of winter reindeer, with cow and mouse rods as controls, at 5, 15, 25 and 37 °C in total darkness.
- **Model:** Poisson regression of event rate, λ(T) = A·exp(−Eₐ/kT) + f.
- **Null:** H₀ is f = 0, tested by one-sided likelihood ratio.
- **Controls:**
  - Recordings are scored blind.
  - Some traces have known numbers of events injected by calibrated dim flashes, to measure detection efficiency.
  - Bleached rods give the electronic false-event rate.
- **Sample size:** about 100 rods for 10⁴ seconds each at 5 °C, giving 10⁶ rod-seconds. Thermal events there should be roughly 60 times rarer than at 37 °C, so about 100 are expected.
- **Sensitivity:** this detects f ≳ 3×10⁻⁵ per second per rod at 95% confidence. That corresponds to ε ≳ 10⁻⁸ to 10⁻⁷, seven or more orders weaker than existing limits.
- **Existing data:** published dark-noise series for toad and primate rods already allow the same fit.

## What would count against it
- **No floor:** f consistent with zero means no detection, though this cannot exclude ε below about 10⁻⁸.
- **Wrong signature:** a floor that changes with shielding, or differs between species with equal pigment cross-section, points to radioactivity or cosmic rays.
- **Already against it:** the lens is irrelevant to this field, so nothing singles out reindeer. At allowed ε a retina sees less than one event per millennium, so no behavioural effect is possible.

--- critique:experimentalist · claude (claude CLI) ---
**1. Weakest link: the floor f is not identifiable from the Arrhenius fit.**
- **Degeneracy:** f is degenerate with any weakly temperature-dependent process, and rod dark noise is not known to be single-exponential down to 5 °C.
- **Stability:** mammalian rods at 5 °C lose transduction gain and rarely stay stable for 10⁴ s. Detection efficiency therefore drifts with temperature, in the same direction as the term being fitted.
- **Cross-section:** σ is about 3–4× too high. The 340 nm β-band is roughly a quarter of the 500 nm peak, and quantum yield is 0.67.
- **Per-rod rate:** with about 10⁸ rhodopsins per rod, the rate is about 10¹⁰·ε² per second, so ε ≳ 5×10⁻⁸ is the realistic reach.

**2. Ordinary explanations to exclude.**
- **Stray photons:** f = 3×10⁻⁵ per second per rod equals about 3×10³ photons per cm² per second. That means light leaks, infrared-viewer leakage, and tissue or Ringer chemiluminescence.
- **Radioactivity:** ⁴⁰K and Cherenkov light in glass and saline.
- **Pigment state:** free opsin or a thermally unstable pigment subpopulation.
- **Scoring artefacts:** continuous-noise excursions scored as events, with a temperature-dependent false-positive rate. The bleached-rod control must be run at each temperature.

**3. Concrete change: replace the fit with a modulated signal.**
- **Mirror:** a conducting surface converts the dark-photon field into real photons of energy m, emitted perpendicular to it. Put the retina at the centre of curvature of a 1 m² spherical aluminium mirror.
- **Gain:** about 10⁵ in rate per rod by my estimate, so about 300× in ε.
- **Null test:** an opaque shutter, opened and closed in blind randomised blocks, switches the signal. H₀ is equal rates open and closed, tested by paired Poisson comparison at one temperature.
- **Filter control:** a 320–350 nm bandpass filter should pass the signal and a long-pass filter should kill it.
- **Preparation:** use toad rods, which are cold-tolerant, stable for hours, and hold about 3×10⁹ rhodopsins.

Photomultiplier dish experiments (FUNK) already reach ε ≈ 10⁻¹² here, as I recall, so the rod version is a biological cross-check only.

--- critique:theorist · claude (claude CLI) ---
**1. Weakest link: the bounds and the floor fit, not the rate.**
- **Rate holds.** 300·ε² per second per molecule is right, because absorption goes as ε²·n·σ·c. The β-band cross-section near 340 nm is about 4×10⁻¹⁷ cm², and in-medium mixing costs another factor of about 3, so the true rate is roughly 10 times lower.
- **Bounds are looser than stated.** From memory, unchecked: solar limits give ε ≲ 10⁻¹², the FUNK mirror search about 10⁻¹² over 2.5–7 eV, and silicon CCDs about 10⁻¹³ to 10⁻¹⁴. The gap is still 4–6 orders in ε.
- **The floor is not identifiable.** With about 100 events, f is degenerate with Eₐ, and scoring efficiency falls as responses slow at 5 °C.
- **The shielding prediction is wrong.** Conductors do not screen at this mass; metal surfaces emit real photons of energy m, at about ε²·3×10¹⁸ per cm² per second.

**2. Ordinary explanations to exclude.**
- Non-Arrhenius thermal pathways, such as a deprotonated Schiff-base subpopulation.
- Cherenkov and radioluminescence from ⁴⁰K and muons.
- Lipid-peroxidation chemiluminescence.
- Continuous phosphodiesterase noise misscored as discrete events.
- Temperature-dependent detection efficiency and rod rundown.

**3. One change: replace the Arrhenius floor with an on/off difference at fixed temperature.**
- **Setup:** a 1 m² spherical aluminium mirror focuses its converted photons onto a 1 mm² retinal patch at the centre of curvature.
- **Gain:** about 10⁶ in flux, so 10³ in ε, reaching roughly 10⁻¹¹.
- **Control:** a UV-blocking shutter, alternated blind, removes only the signal.
- **Analysis:** paired Poisson test on shutter-open minus shutter-closed counts; it needs no Arrhenius model.
- **Species test:** only UV-transparent optics pass the converted photons in vivo, and the tapetum is itself a conversion surface. This restores the reindeer-specific prediction.

This still falls about 10 times short of FUNK, so it is a calibration of rods as detectors, not a discovery search.

## Claim
Dark-photon dark matter with m ≈ 3.5–3.9 eV cannot drive reindeer vision at any allowed mixing. What survives is narrower: rod photoreceptors can act as a single-photon readout for dark photons converted to real 320–350 nm photons at a mirror. This is a calibration of rods as detectors, not a discovery search.

## Mechanism
1. **Field (established if such dark matter exists):** about 10⁸ dark photons per cm³.
2. **Conversion:** a conducting surface emits real photons of energy m perpendicular to itself, at about ε²·3×10¹⁸ per cm² per second.
3. **Detection:** the rhodopsin β-band (σ ≈ 4×10⁻¹⁷ cm², quantum yield 0.67) absorbs them, giving a normal single-photon response.
4. **Dropped:** the claim that shielding leaves the signal unchanged; metal is a source, not a screen.

Direct absorption in the pigment still occurs, at about 30·ε² per second per molecule, but is negligible.

## Size of the effect
These are my estimates, and the bounds are recalled, not checked.

- **Bounds:** ε ≲ 10⁻¹² (solar, FUNK) to 10⁻¹³–10⁻¹⁴ (CCDs).
- **Mirror signal:** a 1 m² mirror gives 3×10²²·ε² photons per second. Focused on 1 mm² of toad retina (about 3×10⁴ rods, about 25% converted to events), that is about 3×10¹⁷·ε² events per second per rod.
- **Noise:** about 0.02 thermal events per second per rod.
- **In vivo:** at ε = 10⁻¹², a reindeer eye gets about 10⁻⁵ events per second against about 10⁶ thermal, so no behavioural effect.

## What we would observe
With a shutter between mirror and retina, the event rate is higher open than closed. The excess passes a 320–350 nm bandpass filter, dies under a long-pass filter, and vanishes when the retina is moved off the centre of curvature.

## How to test it
- **Design:** toad rods at one fixed temperature, with the shutter switched in blind, randomised 100 s blocks.
- **Null:** H₀ is equal open and closed rates, tested by a within-rod permutation test on block labels. This is robust to drift and between-rod overdispersion and needs no Arrhenius model.
- **Controls:**
  - Bandpass and long-pass filters.
  - Off-focus position, which keeps stray light but removes the signal.
  - Bleached rods, for electronic false events.
  - Injected calibrated 340 nm flashes, for detection efficiency.
- **Sample size:** 10 rods for 10⁴ seconds each gives about 1,000 background events per arm. The 95% one-sided reach is an excess of about 1.5×10⁻³ per second per rod, so ε ≈ 7×10⁻¹¹.
- **Pooled option:** recording the whole patch (ganglion cells on an electrode array) could reach about 6×10⁻¹², if pooling efficiency holds.

## What would count against it
- **No excess:** this excludes only ε ≳ 10⁻¹⁰, which is already ruled out.
- **Wrong signature:** an excess that survives the long-pass filter or the off-focus position is stray light, chemiluminescence or Cherenkov light.
- **Already against it:** the reindeer link is essentially gone. UV-transparent optics would only matter for photons from external conductors, and nature provides no focusing mirror. The Arrhenius floor fit is dropped as unidentifiable.

## Cheapest first experiment
Run an A/A test with no large mirror: toad rods on an existing suction rig, with a dummy shutter opaque in both states, blind block labels and injected 340 nm flashes. It checks that the permutation test's false-positive rate is nominal and measures scoring efficiency and the stray-photon floor. If the null is not clean there, nothing further is worth building.
